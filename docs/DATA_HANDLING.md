# CyberFeedback — Data Handling

*For the organization considering an assessment. Written to be handed over as-is.*

You are being asked to let your staff answer candid questions about their own
security habits. The reasonable first question is what happens to those
answers. This document answers it precisely.

---

## What is collected

For each participant, one record containing:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Their selected answer to each multiple-choice question | *"I use a password manager but sometimes write down passwords"* |
| The category each question belongs to | *Password & Access Management* |
| A random identifier generated by the server | `9f2c...` (no link to a person) |
| Submission timestamp | `2026-09-01T14:22:07Z` |
| Consent record | version of the consent text agreed to, and when |
| Language | `en` or `et` |

## What is never collected

**No name. No email address. No employee number. No job title. No department.
No free-text field of any kind.**

There is nowhere in the questionnaire to type a name, because there is no
free-text input at all — every question is multiple choice. The application
never asks a participant to identify themselves and stores no identifier that
could be matched back to a person.

Participant IP addresses are not stored with submissions. If the service runs
behind a web server or proxy, that component may keep its own access logs;
those are standard web infrastructure logs and are not linked to assessment
answers.

---

## Who can see what

There are three distinct roles, and the separation is enforced by the software,
not by policy or good intentions.

**Each participant** sees their own individual report and nothing else. It is
delivered through a one-time link held only by their own browser.

**Your leadership** sees:
- the anonymous, team-wide aggregate,
- their own organizational self-assessment,
- the combined report comparing the two.

Leadership **cannot** see any individual's answers or any individual's report.
The credential needed to open a private report is never issued to a leadership
account, and the participation view returns only counts to that role.

**The operator** (the person running the assessment for you) administers
campaigns and produces your reports. The admin console has no button to open an
individual participant's report. Because submissions carry no name, an
individual record could not be attributed to a person even if it were opened.

### The minimum-group rule

No leadership-facing breakdown is produced for fewer than **5 participants**.
Below that threshold the aggregate is withheld rather than shown, because in a
group of three an "anonymous average" identifies people. This is applied
automatically and cannot be turned off from the interface.

---

## Where the data lives

Assessment data is stored as files on the server operating the service, in a
directory readable only by the application's own account. It is not sent to any
analytics service, advertising network, or third-party database.

The single external service involved is **OpenAI**, which generates the written
recommendations in each report.

### What is sent to OpenAI

A summary derived from the answers: category scores, the selected answer texts,
and the identified gaps. The organization's name is not included in the prompt,
and no participant identifier is included, because neither exists in the data
being summarized.

## Retention and deletion

Data is kept for the duration of the engagement and any agreed follow-up
comparison, then deleted.

You can request deletion at any time. The operator runs a single command that
removes every submission, every generated PDF, and every campaign record for
your organization, and invalidates the assessment links. It is not a
soft-delete or an archive flag — the files are removed from disk.

Ask the operator to perform it in front of you if you want to see it happen.

---

## Honest limitations

Stated plainly, because you would find them anyway and they change how you
should treat the service:

- **Encryption at rest is the host's, not the application's.** The application
  does not encrypt the files it writes. It relies on full-disk encryption of the
  server. Ask the operator to confirm that the host has it enabled.
- **Transport security depends on deployment.** The service must be run behind
  HTTPS. Ask the operator to confirm the URL your staff receive begins with
  `https://`.
- **Anyone holding a participant link can submit a response.** Links are long
  random values and are not guessable, but they are not tied to an individual —
  that is a deliberate trade-off for anonymity. It means participation counts
  can be inflated by someone forwarding the link, and it means the assessment
  should not be treated as an audited headcount.
- **Backups are the operator's responsibility** and may not be encrypted
  separately. Ask.

---

## Questions worth asking the operator

1. Is full-disk encryption enabled on the server holding our data?
2. What is the exact URL our staff will receive, and is it HTTPS?
3. What is your OpenAI data-retention posture, in writing, as of today?
4. Who else has administrative access to the deployment?
5. What is the agreed deletion date, and will you confirm deletion in writing?

A vendor who cannot answer all five specifically is not ready to hold your
employees' candid admissions about security.
